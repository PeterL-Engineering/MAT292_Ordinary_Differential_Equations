\begin{abstract}

    This study solves the Hodgkin-Huxley equations using three explicit numerical methods: Forward Euler (FE), Improved Euler (IE), and fourth-order Runge-Kutta (RK4).
    Gaussian noise, amplitude modulation, and physiological artefacts are injected into the simulated signals to emulate experimental extracellular recordings.
    We filter the resulting data with a 58th-order Finite-Impulse-Response (FIR) filter and perform spike detection via adaptive thresholding and refractory period enforcement.
    The numerical methods converge reliably with a minimum of 14 integration steps per millisecond.
    The filtration stage recovers neural spikes from high-noise conditions, demonstrating a linear relationship between input noise level and output root-mean-square error.

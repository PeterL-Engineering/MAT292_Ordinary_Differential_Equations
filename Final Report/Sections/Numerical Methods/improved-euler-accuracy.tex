Applying rigorous LTE analysis to Improved Euler for Hodgkin-Huxley requires the vector Taylor expansion:

\[
\mathbf{y}(t_{n+1}) = \mathbf{y}(t_n) + h\mathbf{f}(t_n, \mathbf{y}_n) + \frac{h^2}{2}\mathbf{f}'(t_n, \mathbf{y}_n) + \frac{h^3}{6}\mathbf{f}''(t_n, \mathbf{y}_n) + \mathcal{O}(h^4)
\]

where the total derivatives involve the Jacobian $\mathbf{J} = \frac{\partial \mathbf{f}}{\partial \mathbf{y}}$:

\[
\mathbf{f}' = \mathbf{f}_t + \mathbf{J}\mathbf{f}
\]
\[
\mathbf{f}'' = \mathbf{f}_{tt} + 2\mathbf{J}\mathbf{f}_t + \mathbf{J}^2\mathbf{f} + \frac{\partial \mathbf{J}}{\partial \mathbf{y}}\mathbf{f}\mathbf{f}
\]

For Hodgkin-Huxley with $\mathbf{y} = [V, m, h, n]^T$, the Jacobian has complex structure:

\[
\mathbf{J} = 
\left[\begin{array}{cccc}
\dfrac{\partial F}{\partial V} & \dfrac{\partial F}{\partial m} & \dfrac{\partial F}{\partial h} & \dfrac{\partial F}{\partial n} \\[2ex]
\dfrac{\partial G_m}{\partial V} & \dfrac{\partial G_m}{\partial m} & 0 & 0 \\[2ex]
\dfrac{\partial G_h}{\partial V} & 0 & \dfrac{\partial G_h}{\partial h} & 0 \\[2ex]
\dfrac{\partial G_n}{\partial V} & 0 & 0 & \dfrac{\partial G_n}{\partial n}
\end{array}\right]
\]

The Improved Euler method matches the Taylor expansion up to $h^1$ but has error in the $h^2$ term:

\[
\mathbf{y}_{n+1}^{IE} = \mathbf{y}_n + h\mathbf{f} + \frac{h^2}{2}\mathbf{J}\mathbf{f} + \mathcal{O}(h^3)
\]

The LTE emerges from the $h^2$ and $h^3$ term mismatches:

\[
\text{LTE} = \mathbf{y}(t_{n+1}) - \mathbf{y}_{n+1}^{IE} = \frac{h^2}{2}(\mathbf{f}_t + \frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial \mathbf{J}}{\partial \mathbf{y}}\mathbf{f}\mathbf{f}) + \mathcal{O}(h^3)
\]

For Hodgkin-Huxley, the complex nonlinear couplings mean the LTE depends on time derivatives of the currents and higher-order Jacobian terms. Nevertheless, the asymptotic order $\mathcal{O}(h^2)$ is preserved.

Thus Improved Euler remains second-order for Hodgkin-Huxley, though the LTE constant depends on the full nonlinear structure through the Jacobian and its derivatives.

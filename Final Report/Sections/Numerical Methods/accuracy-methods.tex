The accuracy of numerical methods for ODEs is characterized by the \textit{local truncation error} (LTE), defined as the error in a single step assuming the previous step was exact. For a true solution \(\mathbf{y}(t)\) and a numerical approximation \(\mathbf{y}_n\), the LTE at \(t_{n+1}\) is:
\[
\text{LTE} = \mathbf{y}(t_{n+1}) - \mathbf{y}_{n+1}.
\]
The order of a method is the lowest power of the step size \(h\) in the LTE after simplification.

For the vector-valued Hodgkin-Huxley equations, \(\mathbf{y} = [V, m, h, n]^T\), the analysis requires the Taylor expansion:
\[
\mathbf{y}(t_{n+1}) = \mathbf{y}(t_n) + h\mathbf{f} + \frac{h^2}{2}\mathbf{f}' + \frac{h^3}{6}\mathbf{f}'' + \frac{h^4}{24}\mathbf{f}''' + \mathcal{O}(h^5),
\]
where the total derivatives involve the Jacobian \(\mathbf{J} = \frac{\partial \mathbf{f}}{\partial \mathbf{y}}\), e.g., \(\mathbf{f}' = \mathbf{f}_t + \mathbf{J}\mathbf{f}\).

\subsubsection{Euler's Method}
\label{subsubsec:euler-method-accuracy}

Euler's update, \(\mathbf{y}_{n+1} = \mathbf{y}_n + h\mathbf{f}\), matches only the first term of the Taylor expansion. The LTE is:
\[
\text{LTE} = \frac{h^2}{2}\mathbf{f}' + \mathcal{O}(h^3) = \frac{h^2}{2}(\mathbf{f}_t + \mathbf{J}\mathbf{f}) + \mathcal{O}(h^3).
\]
Thus, Euler's method is first-order. The LTE constant depends on the system dynamics through the Jacobian.

\subsubsection{Improved Euler's Method}
\label{subsubsec:improved-euler-accuracy}
The Improved Euler method matches the Taylor expansion up to the \(h^1\) term but has an error in the \(h^2\) term. Its LTE is:
\[
\text{LTE} = \frac{h^2}{2}\mathbf{f}_t + \mathcal{O}(h^3).
\]
The method remains second-order (\(\mathcal{O}(h^2)\)) for Hodgkin-Huxley, with the LTE constant depending on the time derivative of the vector field.

\subsubsection{Runge-Kutta Method}
\label{subsubsec:runge-kutta-accuracy}
The RK4 method is designed to match the Taylor expansion up to the \(h^4\) term. The leading error term is:
\[
\text{LTE} = -\frac{h^5}{720}\mathbf{f}^{(4)}(\xi_n) + \mathcal{O}(h^6).
\]
Thus, RK4 is fourth-order (\(\mathcal{O}(h^5)\)) for Hodgkin-Huxley, though the LTE constant involves high-order derivatives of the system's nonlinear structure.

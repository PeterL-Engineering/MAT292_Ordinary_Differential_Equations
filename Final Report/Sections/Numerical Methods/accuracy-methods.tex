When evaluating the accuracy of numerical methods for ordinary differential equations, error can be characterized over a single step or over the entire time interval.
These are known as the \textit{local truncation error} (LTE) and the \textit{global truncation error} (GTE), respectively.
While the GTE describes the cumulative error at a final time, the LTE provides a localized measure of the consistency of a method.

The LTE is defined as the error incurred in a single step under the key assumption that the previous step was exact.
In its general form, it is found by subtracting the numerical update from the exact solution after one step.
Mathematically, for a true solution \(\phi(t)\) and a numerical approximation \(y_n\), the LTE at time \(t_{n+1}\) is given by:

\[
\text{LTE} = \phi(t_{n+1}) - y_{n+1}.
\]

Substituting the Taylor series expansion of the exact solution around \(t_n\) yields the working form:

\[
\text{LTE} = \left[ \phi(t_n) + h\phi'(t_n) + \frac{h^2}{2!}\phi''(t_n) + \frac{h^3}{3!}\phi'''(t_n) + \cdots \right] - y_{n+1},
\]

where \(h\) is the step size.
The order of the method is determined by the lowest power of \(h\) that remains in this expression after the terms from the numerical method \(y_{n+1}\) are substituted and simplified.

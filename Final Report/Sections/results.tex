\subsection{Spike Detection}
\label{subsec:spike-detection-results}

    Even in the presence of significant noise, action potential spikes are reliably detected in the FIR-filtered signal and the spike times align with the peaks of the membrane potential. 
    The detection theshold ensures true spikes are captured while ignoring noise fluctuations.
    The spike raster plot and inter-spike interval (ISI) histogram (Figure $\ref{fig:spike-detection-plots}$) confirm temporal distribution of spikes within expectation of the model's firing patterns.
    ISI is valuable in determining if a detector is over- or under-sensitive.
    If there are false positives, the ISI distribution would shorter intervals.
    For missed spikes that are not at the beginning or end, the ISI distribution would show slow firing. 
    
    In this plot, the spike raster shows four detected spikes at 28.65 ms, 42.75 ms, 55.40 ms, and 69.35 ms. 
    Computing the inter-spike intervals using np.diff yields values of 14.10 ms, 12.65 ms, and 13.95 ms. 
    Although visually, the ISI appears to show uneven spacing due to scale, the histogram correctly shows a nearly periodic firing pattern.
    All intervals are clustered between 12-14 ms.
    This consistency confirms that spike detection is internally coherent.

    The spike times were compared with the ground truth using a $\pm$3 m/s window. 
    The detector produced 4 spikes, all of which matched ground truth within the window (true positives). 
    One event at 69.35 ms did not match the ground truth spike located at 66.35 ms within a stricter 2 ms window, under that requirement it would be considered a false positive (and the ground-truth spike a false negative).
    With the more permissive ±3 ms window, the detection is counted as a true positive with 3.0 ms jitter. 
    Therefore the classification of this spike depends on the timing error tolerance, here results are reported for both tolerance values to show sensitivity to timing jitter.

    Using the $\pm$3 m/s window, the sensitivity (true positive rate) $TP\text/(TP + FN)$ is 80$\%$, showing strong sensitivity to action potential spikes.

Accurate spike detection in noisy extracellular recordings is essential for brain-computer 
interfaces and fundamental neuroscience. 
The Hodgkin-Huxley (H-H) equations serve as a foundational biophysical model for this task, 
describing the ionic conductance dynamics that generate neural action potentials.

Neural signal processing supports critical applications beyond EEG and microelectrode arrays, 
including epileptic seizure prediction \citep{addai-domfe2024epileptic} and adaptive deep brain 
stimulation for Parkinson's disease \citep{aljalal2022parkinson}. 
The increasing use of high-density neural probes further motivates the need for efficient, 
real-time filtering algorithms to process large-scale data streams \citep{ye2024ultra}.

This work implements a simulation pipeline with three stages:
(i) generating ground-truth neural signals by solving the H-H equations with explicit numerical methods,
(ii) injecting realistic noise and physiological artefacts into the signal,
and (iii) applying real-time filtration and spike detection to the corrupted data.

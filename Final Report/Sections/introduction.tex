Accurate spike detection from noisy extracellular recordings is essential for brain-computer interfaces and neuroscience.
The Hodgkin-Huxley (H-H) equations provide a biophysical model for neural action potentials by describing their underlying ionic conductance dynamics.

Beyond EEG and microelectrode arrays, neural signal processing enables critical applications including epileptic seizure prediction \citep{addai-domfe2024epileptic} and adaptive deep brain stimulation for Parkinson's disease \citep{aljalal2022parkinson}.
The proliferation of high-density neural probes further motivates efficient filtering solutions for large-scale data streams \citep{ye2024ultra}.

Our work comprises three simulation stages:
(i) data generation via explicit numerical methods,
(ii) noise injection to emulate realistic conditions,
and (iii) real-time filtration and spike detection.

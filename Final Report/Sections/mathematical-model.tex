The Hodgkin-Huxley model comprises a system of nonlinear differential equations that capture the propagation of action potentials in neurons through three distinct ionic currents: sodium, potassium, and a leak current.  
We represent the flow of these ions using an equivalent electrical circuit, which provides a framework for equations describing the cell's electrical properties \citep{Schwiening2012}.

\subsection{Model Components and Equations}
\label{subsec:hh-equations}

\begin{minipage}{\linewidth}
    \label{fig:ion-circuit}
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.8\linewidth]{Figs/ion_circuit.png}
    \captionof{figure}{Circuit representation of ion transfer across the cell membrane \citep{Gerstner2014}}
\end{minipage}

This circuit model maps each aspect of ionic transfer to an electrical component: ion channels become resistors, ionic concentration gradients correspond to batteries, and the lipid bilayer functions as a capacitor \cite{Gerstner2014}.  
We combine these elements according to Figure~\ref{fig:ion-circuit} to derive the total membrane current equation:
\begin{dmath}
    I = C_{M}\frac{dV}{dt} + \bar{g}_{K}n^4(V - V_{K}) + \bar{g}_{Na}m^3h(V - V_{Na}) + \bar{g}_{l}(V - V_{l})
    \label{eq:ion-current}
\end{dmath}
In relating Equation~\ref{eq:ion-current} to Figure~\ref{fig:ion-circuit}, we recognize V as analogous to u, while $V_x$ corresponds to $E_x$ (where x represents Na, K, or l).  
The conductance terms $g_x$ reflect the maximum permeability of each channel type, derived from the resistance values in the circuit model.

\subsection{Gating Variables and Activation/Inactivation}
\label{subsec:gating-variables}

Hodgkin and Huxley proposed that specific charged particles must shift under the membrane potential's influence to permit $\mathit{Na}^+$ or $\mathit{K}^+$ movement \citep{Schwiening2012}.  
Their model employs three gating variables ($\mathit{m}$, $\mathit{n}$, and $\mathit{h}$) to represent the instantaneous probability of channel opening.  
For $\mathit{Na}^+$ channels, $\mathit{m}$ describes activation while $\mathit{h}$ captures inactivation; similarly, $\mathit{n}$ governs $\mathit{K}^+$ channel activation.  
They determined that the exponents in $\mathit{n}^4$, $\mathit{m}^3$, and $\mathit{h}$ yielded the best fit to experimental data \citep{Schwiening2012}.  
We describe each gating variable's dynamics with equations of the form:
\begin{dmath}
    \dot{\mathit{x}} = -\frac{1}{\tau_{\mathit{x}}(\mathit{u})}[\mathit{x} - \mathit{x}_{0}(\mathit{u})]
    \label{eq:gating-variables}
\end{dmath}
where $\mathit{x}$ represents $\mathit{m}$, $\mathit{n}$, or $\mathit{h}$, and both $\tau_{\mathit{x}}(\mathit{u})$ and $\mathit{x}_{0}(\mathit{u})$ depend on the instantaneous membrane potential $\mathit{u}$ \citep{Gerstner2014}.
